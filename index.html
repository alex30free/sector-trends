    How the indices are built
    Sector index
    Each sector is an equal-weight index of its stocks, starting at 100 in January 2006. Every week the index moves by the average weekly price change of its stocks, so a 2 kr stock counts as much as a 900 kr stock. Stocks listed later (for example Evolution, Epiroc, Sagax) join from their first full week without making the line jump.
    Prices
    Weekly closing prices (the last trading day of each week) from Yahoo Finance, adjusted by Yahoo for share splits and spin-offs. Dividends are not added back, so it is a price index, like a normal price chart.
    MA40
    The average of the last 40 weekly values, about 200 trading days. Above the MA40 = uptrend, below = downtrend. "Since" is the week the line last crossed its MA40.
    Large, Mid and Small Cap
    Nasdaq's official OMX Stockholm Large Cap, Mid Cap and Small Cap price indices (weekly closes), from Nasdaq's website. Free history goes back 10 years (to November 2016); new weeks are added every Friday. In the chart all three are set to 100 at the start of the chosen period.
    OMXSPI
    OMX Stockholm All-Share (price index): every share on Nasdaq Stockholm, weighted by market value. Shown with Large, Mid and Small Cap and used for the bull-trend check. If it has no data, the check uses Large Cap instead.
    Bull-trend check
    Six signals, each worth 1 point (breadth between 40% and 60% gives ½): (1) index above its MA40; (2) MA40 higher than 4 weeks ago; (3) index higher than 52 weeks ago (12-month momentum); (4) higher highs and higher lows: the highest and the lowest weekly close of the last 13 weeks are both above those of the 13 weeks before; (5) at least 60% of the stocks above their own MA40; (6) the advance/decline line above its MA40. 5–6 points with the index above its MA40 = robust bull trend. The history line counts every past week with the same score and checks the index 26 weeks later. The periods overlap, so it is a rough guide, not a proof.
    Market breadth
    Calculated from the page's own stocks, the larger Swedish companies chosen today, not all stocks on the exchange. Stocks that were delisted, like those that went bankrupt, are missing, which flatters the old history a little. A breadth thrust here means the share above MA40 rising from under 30% to over 70% within 10 weeks. That is a weekly version of Martin Zweig's daily signal, so it is not the same thing.
    Caveat
    Moving-average signals lag and can flip back and forth in sideways markets. This is an overview tool, not a buy or sell signal.

    Statistik för OMXSPI i bull- och björntrendOMXSPI statistics in bull and bear trends

    Årlig utveckling

    Staplarna visar hur mycket OMXSPI steg varje år, men bara räknat på de dagar då indexet låg över sitt 200-dagars glidande medelvärde. Dagar under MA200 är bortplockade. Därför finns inga negativa år, och träffsäkerheten är 100 %.

    Den blå linjen (höger axel) visar den sammanlagda utvecklingen om man bara hade ägt index under dessa dagar: omkring 17 000 % sedan 1997, före avgifter och skatt. Det är ingen handelsstrategi. I verkligheten förlorar man en del vid varje köp och sälj runt MA200, och man missar ofta början på uppgångarna.

    • Snitt 20,3 % · median 17,5 %: ett typiskt år gav indexet 17–20 % under bulldagarna. Snittet är högre än medianen eftersom några extremår, som 1999 med cirka +75 %, drar upp det.
    • Stdev 14,6 %: standardavvikelsen, alltså hur mycket åren brukar avvika från snittet. Ett vanligt år hamnar ungefär mellan +6 % och +35 %.
    • Histogrammet visar hur många år som hamnat i varje intervall. De flesta ligger mellan cirka 6 % och 35 %.

    Genomsnittlig utveckling per månad (tabellen)

    • Snitt / Median: genomsnittlig respektive mittersta utveckling för månaden.
    • Hitratio: andelen gånger som månaden slutade plus.
    • Antal: hur många gånger månaden ingår i statistiken, cirka 21–25 sedan 1997.
    • Stdev: hur mycket utfallen spretar.
    • Geno…: rubriken är avkortad. Talet är genomsnittet för de gånger månaden gick upp. Det framgår av december, där hitratio är 100 % och talet blir samma som snittet.
    • Störst…: den största uppgången för månaden.

    Tolkning: december är starkast, +4,1 % i snitt och upp alla 22 gånger. Därefter kommer februari (+3,2 %), juli (+2,9 %) och november (+2,8 %, upp 87 % av gångerna). Maj (+0,2 %), augusti (+0,7 %) och juni (+0,8 %) är svagast, vilket stämmer med det kända mönstret "sell in May". Skillnaderna mellan månaderna (1–4 %) är dock små jämfört med spridningen (cirka 3 %), och varje månad har bara drygt 20 observationer. Se det som en svag tendens, inte en regel.

    Yearly performance

    The bars show how much OMXSPI rose each year, counting only the days when the index was above its 200-day moving average. Days below the MA200 are left out, so there are no negative years and the hit ratio is 100%.

    The blue line (right axis) is the cumulative result of owning the index only on those days: about 17,000% since 1997, before costs and tax. It is not a trading strategy. In practice you lose something on every buy and sell around the MA200, and you often miss the start of a rally.

    • Average 20.3% · median 17.5%: a typical year gained 17–20% on its bull days. The average is higher than the median because a few extreme years, such as 1999 at about +75%, pull it up.
    • Stdev 14.6%: the standard deviation, i.e. how far years usually land from the average. A normal year falls roughly between +6% and +35%.
    • The histogram counts how many years fell in each range. Most are between about 6% and 35%.

    Average performance by month (the table)

    • Average / Median: the mean and the middle result for the month.
    • Hit ratio: the share of times the month ended positive.
    • Count: how many times the month is in the sample, about 21–25 since 1997.
    • Stdev: how spread out the results are.
    • Geno…: the heading is cut off. The figure is the average of the times the month rose. December shows this: with a 100% hit ratio, it equals the average.
    • Störst…: the month's largest gain.

    Reading: December is strongest, +4.1% on average and up all 22 times. Next come February (+3.2%), July (+2.9%) and November (+2.8%, up 87% of the time). May (+0.2%), August (+0.7%) and June (+0.8%) are weakest, which fits the well-known "sell in May" pattern. The gaps between months (1–4%) are small compared with the spread (about 3%), though, and each month has only about 20 observations. Treat it as a weak tendency, not a rule.

    Månatlig utveckling

    Varje stapel är en månad under bulltrend: grön om indexet steg, röd om det föll. Den blå linjen är den sammanlagda utvecklingen.

    • Snitt 1,95 % · median 1,70 %: en vanlig bullmånad ger knappt 2 %.
    • Hitratio 72 %: ungefär 7 av 10 månader slutar plus.
    • Stdev 3,5 %: en enskild månad kan lätt avvika med 3–4 procentenheter från snittet.
    • Upp 3,47 % (max 16,5 %) · Ner −2,05 % (max −7,0 %): uppmånaderna är i snitt större än nedmånaderna, och de är dessutom fler. Det är därifrån avkastningen i bulltrend kommer.
    • Histogrammet lutar åt höger: de flesta månader ligger mellan 0 och +5 %, och ras på över −5 % är ovanliga.

    Genomsnittlig utveckling per dag i månaden

    Här visas snittet för varje kalenderdag (1–31). Menyn "Alla" väljer vilka månader som räknas med.

    Tolkning: den 1:a sticker ut med +0,52 % i snitt och plus 71 % av gångerna. Den 2:a–4:e är också positiva. Det kallas månadsskifteseffekten: löner, pensionsinbetalningar och månadssparande i fonder köper aktier kring månadsskiftet. Mitt i månaden är snittet nära noll (den 10:e −0,01 %). Den 1:a har färre observationer (140) eftersom den ofta faller på en helg eller helgdag.

    Monthly performance

    Each bar is one month in a bull trend: green if the index rose, red if it fell. The blue line is the cumulative result.

    • Average 1.95% · median 1.70%: a normal bull month gives just under 2%.
    • Hit ratio 72%: about 7 in 10 months end positive.
    • Stdev 3.5%: a single month can easily differ by 3–4 points from the average.
    • Up 3.47% (max 16.5%) · Down −2.05% (max −7.0%): up months are bigger on average than down months, and there are more of them. That is where the return in a bull trend comes from.
    • The histogram leans to the right: most months fall between 0 and +5%, and drops worse than −5% are rare.

    Average performance by day of the month

    The average for each calendar day (1–31). The "Alla" (All) menu chooses which months are included.

    Reading: the 1st stands out at +0.52% on average, positive 71% of the time. The 2nd–4th are also positive. This is the turn-of-the-month effect: salaries, pension contributions and monthly fund savings buy shares around the turn of the month. Mid-month the average is close to zero (the 10th is −0.01%). The 1st has fewer observations (140) because it often falls on a weekend or holiday.

    Daglig utveckling

    • Snitt 0,11 % · median 0,13 % per dag: det låter lite, men med cirka 170 bulldagar per år (ungefär 5 000 sedan 1997) blir det ungefär de 20 % per år som syns i årsfliken.
    • Hitratio 56 %: drygt varannan dag är en plusdag.
    • Stdev 1,0 %: en vanlig dag rör sig indexet upp till ungefär ±1 %.
    • Upp 0,75 % · Ner −0,73 %: plus- och minusdagar är i snitt lika stora. Fördelen i bulltrend kommer alltså av att det är fler plusdagar (56 mot 44), inte av att de är större.
    • Histogrammet liknar en klockkurva runt noll. De höga staplarna längst ut till vänster och höger samlar alla dagar utanför skalan, under −3,2 % respektive över +3,2 %.

    Genomsnittlig utveckling per veckodag

    Tolkning: fredag är bäst (+0,18 %, plus 60 % av gångerna) och tisdag svagast (+0,05 %). Varje veckodag har cirka 1 000 observationer, totalt ungefär 5 000 bulldagar sedan 1997. Skillnaderna mellan veckodagarna (någon tiondels procent) är dock små jämfört med den dagliga spridningen på 1 %, och de försvinner nästan helt efter courtage och spread. Det är intressant att veta, men inget att handla på.

    Daily performance

    • Average 0.11% · median 0.13% per day: it sounds small, but over about 170 bull days a year (roughly 5,000 since 1997) it adds up to the roughly 20% a year seen on the Year tab.
    • Hit ratio 56%: slightly more than every other day is an up day.
    • Stdev 1.0%: on a normal day the index moves up to about ±1%.
    • Up 0.75% · Down −0.73%: up and down days are about the same size. The edge in a bull trend comes from there being more up days (56 vs 44), not bigger ones.
    • The histogram is a bell curve around zero. The tall bars at the far left and right collect all days beyond the scale, below −3.2% and above +3.2%.

    Average performance by weekday

    Reading: Friday is best (+0.18%, up 60% of the time) and Tuesday weakest (+0.05%). Each weekday has about 1,000 observations, roughly 5,000 bull days since 1997 in all. The gaps between weekdays (a tenth of a percent or so) are small compared with the daily spread of 1%, though, and almost disappear after brokerage fees and spread. Interesting to know, but nothing to trade on.

    Yearly performance

    Only the days when OMXSPI was below its 200-day moving average are counted. The bars show how much the index fell or rose on those days each year.

    The blue line shows the result of owning the index only on bear days: about −97% since 1997. In a bull trend the same line was about +17,000%.

    • Average −10.5% · median −6.8%: bear days cost just over 10% a year on average. A few disaster years pull the average down, above all 2008 (about −42%) and 2002 (about −38%).
    • Hit ratio 7%: in only 2 of 28 years were the bear days positive overall, and then by at most +6%.
    • Stdev 11.1%: the years differ a lot. Some years it is a small dip of a few percent, others a crash.

    Average performance by month (the table)

    The columns mean the same as on the bull-trend tab. Note the count: each month has only 10–18 observations, because bear trends are rarer. The month-by-month figures are therefore very uncertain.

    Reading: September is clearly worst, −6.4% on average and up only 1 in 13 times. Then come March (−4.0%), June (−4.0%), December (−3.5%) and August (−3.1%). Only April (+2.1%), November (+1.6%) and October (+0.3%) are positive on average. Even there the median was negative in October and November, so a few big gains lift the average. These are often the sharp turns at the end of a bear market, as in autumn 2002 and 2022. December is the strongest month in a bull trend but weak in a bear trend. Seasonal patterns depend more on the trend than on the calendar.

    Conclusion: almost all of the Stockholm exchange's return has come in bull trends, and almost all of the big losses in bear trends. In bear trends the swings are also nearly twice as large. That is the classic argument for a trend filter such as the MA200. The calendar patterns (month, day of month, weekday) are small and often flip sign between bull and bear. The trend matters far more than the calendar.

    But: the statistics assume you know the trend on the same day. In practice you switch the day after the price crosses the MA200. Near the line there are many false signals, and every switch costs brokerage and spread. The real gap between following the trend and simply holding the index is therefore much smaller than the blue lines suggest. Historically, the main benefit has been avoiding the long crashes of 2001–2002 and 2008.
